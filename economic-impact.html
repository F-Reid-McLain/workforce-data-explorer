Two Ways to Measure Economic Impact

Location Quotient and Economic Impact Index answer different questions about an industry. Understanding both reveals which sectors are true anchors — and which are large without being transformative.

LQ

Location Quotient

Measures how concentrated a sector is in Macon relative to the national average. An LQ above 1.0 means Macon employs a disproportionately large share of workers in that sector compared to the US as a whole — a local specialty.

LQ = (Macon employment share) ÷ (national employment share)
LQ > 1.0 — sector is more concentrated here than nationally
LQ = 1.0 — matches the national industry mix
LQ < 1.0 — underrepresented locally
EII

Economic Impact Index

Measures the per-job economic quality of a sector — how much above or below the regional wage baseline it sits, adjusted for its growth trajectory. EII above 1.0 means the average job in this sector pays better than the regional norm and/or is growing.

EII = (avg sector wage ÷ regional avg wage) × (1 + growth rate)
EII > 1.0 — above-average economic value per job
EII = 1.0 — matches the regional wage baseline
EII < 1.0 — below-average economic value per job
